The result

North Carolina got the win it needed. The Tar Heels beat UConn 38-21 on a rainy Saturday at Rentschler Field, snapped a two-game losing streak and moved to 5-4 (2-3 ACC). That leaves them one win from bowl eligibility with three games to play, and two wins from seven.

UNC never trailed. It led 7-0 on a first-quarter pick-six, gave up a tie early in the second, then scored the next three touchdowns and went to the half up 28-14. UConn got within a touchdown in the third quarter and was scoreless over the final 25 minutes. The Huskies punted twice all day, but they also turned the ball over three times and went 1 for 4 on fourth down, and that is the game.

This one looked different from the last two. The Tar Heels lost 31-30 in overtime to Syracuse and 38-35 to Miami, and both losses came with the defense giving up more than 400 yards. UConn got 368, and Carolina took the ball away three times after going two games without a takeaway. Bill Belichick said afterward that the team rebounded pretty well after a couple of tough losses, and the scoreboard backed him up.

It was also Belichick's first game back coaching in New England since the Patriots. He played at Wesleyan from 1971 to 1974, about 20 miles from Rentschler Field, and said it was good to be back. There was a footnote on the other sideline: UNC defensive line coach Bob Diaco went 11-26 in three seasons at UConn before he was fired after a 3-9 finish in 2016. His group helped hold the Huskies to 123 rushing yards.

Carolina was favored by 7 to 8.5, so the Tar Heels covered and the game went well over the total with 59 points. I had it 31-17 in the pre-game breakdown and called that the optimistic end of where the game could land. UNC scored seven more than I had and UConn scored four more. I had the winner, the margin and the general shape right and the totals too low.

I also wrote that the weather wasn't a factor, with a forecast in the low 60s. It rained for most of the game, the crowd of 36,901 thinned out once the rain picked up in the second half, and it may be part of why Edwards's throws stayed short. That one is on me.

It was the Tar Heels' third straight road win, and they are 4-1 away from home or at neutral sites this season. The all-time series with UConn now stands at 4-1 in Carolina's favor, and it was the program's 760th victory.

The numbers

Start with the yardage, because it tells you this was closer than the score. UNC outgained UConn 419-368, averaged 5.7 yards a play to UConn's 5.6, and ran 74 plays to 66. Carolina held the ball for roughly 36 minutes to UConn's 24 by my count of the drive chart, and two of its drives ate more than six minutes apiece: a 16-play, 75-yard touchdown march in the second quarter and a 13-play, 30-yard drive in the fourth that ended in a missed field goal.

The separation came from the ball. UNC took it away three times (two interceptions and a fumble recovery) and gave it up once. Those three takeaways produced 21 points: Timir Hickman-Collins's pick-six, Jordan Shipp's 6-yard touchdown catch four plays after the fumble recovery, and Jelani Thurman's 3-yard touchdown catch after Jalon Thompson's interception. UConn got nothing from Billy Edwards's interception, because Jack O'Connor missed a 45-yard field goal wide left.

Take the turnover points out and UConn won the rest of the game 21-17. I don't say that to take anything away from the Tar Heels. The plan was to take the ball away and make the Huskies pay for mistakes, and it worked. But it explains why a game with nearly equal yardage per play ended with a 17-point margin.

One more way to look at it: the offense scored 31 points, which is the number I penciled in for the whole team, and the defense added seven. Edwards had a 153.5 passer rating, the Tar Heels went 9 for 16 on third down and 2 for 2 on fourth, and they scored touchdowns on four of 11 possessions. Those are good numbers for a unit that has had trouble finishing drives all year.

What went right

1

Carolina took the ball away, and it counted

UNC had three takeaways, its season high, and turned them into 21 points. That was my sixth target in the pre-game breakdown and the one that mattered most. Hickman-Collins had a pick-six and a fumble recovery on 15 snaps, which makes him the first Tar Heel with two takeaways in a game since Cedric Gray in 2023, and the pick-six was UNC's first since Power Echols in November 2024. Thompson's interception led to the clinching score.

Hickman-Collins credited the practice routine: turnovers have been a daily drill. Belichick's line was that you have to get them first and then take advantage. Carolina did both.

2

The pass rush finally turned into sacks

UNC sacked Osborne three times, with two from Seelmann and one from Melkart Abou Jaoude, and Donovan Hoilette added another tackle for loss. Osborne finished 17 of 35 with two interceptions and a lost fumble. The Huskies' protection had been a concern all season, and this is the day the Tar Heels cashed it in.

Seelmann's final sack on fourth-and-19 ended UConn's last threat, and his three pass breakups came from a linebacker spot. It was one of the best individual games by a UNC defender this year.

3

The run defense took away UConn's best weapon

UConn came in averaging 190.8 rushing yards and 5.32 a carry. It left with 123 on 31 carries, 4.0 a pop, and a 25-yard scramble by Osborne accounted for most of his 28 net yards. Bowers got 68 yards on 13 carries and a score, Kenji Christian had 27 on seven, and Trey Cornist did not carry at all.

UConn's 31 carries include the three sacks, so its backs got fewer touches than that, and the longest run by a back was 14 yards. Leroy Jackson (five tackles) and Isaiah Johnson (four) led the line in tackles, and the front seven set the tone early.

4

Thurman again, and the tight end offense

Thurman caught seven passes for 64 yards and two touchdowns, tying Mike Chatham's 1979 school record for touchdown catches by a tight end in a season at eight. He is up to 45 catches for 525 yards. He played 65 of 76 snaps, and both of his scores came from 3 yards out.

I called the tight ends the feature of this offense before the game, and the box score supports it. Thurman, Carson Sneed and Shamar Easter caught nine of Edwards's 29 completions.

5

A balanced offense that converted

UNC rushed for 164 yards, 4.6 a carry, and its three backs combined for 156 on 31 carries (5.0). Hall's 39-yarder was the headline, but the more important stats are that the Tar Heels went 9 for 16 on third down and 2 for 2 on fourth..

The 16-play, 75-yard drive before the half was one of the best UNC has put together this season, and it came right after UConn had cut the lead to seven.

6

The Tar Heels did not blow it

A lead slipped away in four games this season, and the Tar Heels never let this one slip. UConn pulled within seven in the third quarter and then went scoreless for the last 25 minutes, with Carolina getting the stops it had not been getting. UConn's last three possessions ended on downs, in an interception and on downs again.

UNC has now scored 30 or more in three straight games after not reaching 30 against an FBS opponent in its first 19 under Belichick..

What went wrong

1

Penalties

UNC was flagged six times for 50 yards, while UConn took three for 27. That is the exact opposite of what I expected. In the pre-game breakdown I listed UConn's 8.5 flags a game as a target, and the Huskies took three, far below their average. Carolina is normally one of the least-penalized teams in the ACC.

The flags were costly. There was a pass interference call, an illegal formation penalty on a punt, and two holding calls on the offensive line that killed drives. They didn't decide this game, but they are the kind of mistakes that do when the margin is thinner.

2

Too many big plays

The defense gave up 13 plays of more than 15 yards, which is a lot for a game where UConn only ran 66 snaps. The Huskies averaged 5.6 yards a play and 14.4 a catch, and they strung together a 93-yard touchdown drive in seven plays, a 75-yard touchdown drive in 1:08 and a 53-yard drive in six plays. Treadway had 82 yards on four catches, Julian Allen had a 33-yarder and Xavier Watson a 28-yarder.

Missed tackles and blown coverages caused most of it, the same problem that showed up against Miami. The Tar Heels held UConn to three touchdowns on 12 possessions, but 368 yards allowed shows the defense is still leaky.

3

The second-half offense went quiet

UNC scored 10 points in the second half, and seven of them came on a 32-yard drive after Thompson's interception. On its own possessions after halftime, the offense had a punt, a field goal, a missed field goal and the short-field touchdown. The 13-play, 30-yard drive that ended with the 49-yard miss is the one that stands out: 6:42 of clock for no points while UConn was still within 10.

It was the same pattern that has hurt Carolina in other games. The offense can score in bunches before the half and then lose its edge. It didn't cost the Tar Heels this time because the defense held, but a closer game would have asked more of the offense.

4

Nobody stretched the field

Edwards completed 29 of 38 passes, but his longest completion was 23 yards, to Demon June. He averaged 6.7 yards an attempt. The wide receivers combined for 11 catches and 104 yards, and Jordan Shipp, with five for 55, was the only one who had more than four.

The pre-game thought was that UNC's size at receiver would win on the outside against UConn's smaller cornerbacks. It didn't show up. The offense got what it needed from the tight ends and the backs, but it left yards on the field.

5

A slow, shaky start

For all the good that came after, the first three possessions went interception, punt, punt, and the Tar Heels led 7-0 only because of a defensive touchdown. Edwards's first-drive interception was his first since Oct. 10 at Pitt, and he missed a few throws he normally makes in the first half. UNC's offense had no touchdown until Hall's run at the 10:05 mark of the second quarter.

A UConn punt pinned UNC at its own 3 on the second of those drives, and it went nowhere. To be fair, the offense scored touchdowns on three straight possessions once the second quarter got going.

Position-group grades

Here is how I'd grade the day. I'm measuring each group against what Carolina needed from it on Saturday and against how it has played all year, and I've used the snap counts to see who actually carried the load. The grades are mine and they are not a curve: a B is a solid day with real flaws, and an A means the group won its matchup and did it convincingly.

Taken together, I'd call it an A- for the team. The defense won the game, the offense did enough in the first half and made its plays in the fourth quarter, and the mistakes were the kind that cost yards and not points. The snap counts show continuity on the offensive line and a lot of rotation on the defensive line, at receiver and in the secondary.

QuarterbacksB+

Billy Edwards Jr. finished 29 of 38 for 255 yards, three touchdowns and one interception, a 153.5 passer rating in the rain. He played all 76 snaps, ran four times for 10 yards and was sacked once. It was his third straight game with multiple touchdown passes, and the interception was his first since Oct. 10 at Pitt.

The interception came on the first drive and gave UConn the ball at the UNC 48, which is the kind of start that can sink a quarterback's day. It didn't. After the first three possessions Carolina scored touchdowns on three straight drives and Edwards was running all of them: Hall's 39-yarder came on a third-and-9 call, the 6-yard touchdown to Shipp came four plays after the fumble recovery, and the 16-play drive before the half ended with his throw to Thurman.

What Edwards did not do was stretch the defense. His longest completion was 23 yards, he averaged 6.7 yards an attempt, and nine of his 29 completions went to running backs, nine to tight ends and 11 to receivers. His three touchdown throws covered 6, 3 and 3 yards. That is the offense working as designed, with quick reads, a lot of tight end usage and not many risks, but it also means Edwards has not had to prove he can win a game on his arm.

He was at his best when it mattered. Carolina went 9 for 16 on third down, and with 5:12 left he hit Shipp for 8 yards on fourth-and-5 to keep alive the drive that put the game away. By my math his season line is now 178 of 273 (65.2 percent) for 2,127 yards, 18 touchdowns and six interceptions.

He gets a B+ and not an A because of the first-drive interception, a few misses in the first half and the absence of anything vertical. The efficiency and decision-making are what the Tar Heels need from him, and they got both.

Running backsB+

Three backs combined for 31 carries, 156 yards and a touchdown. That is 5.0 a carry from a group on a team that came into the game averaging 3.65, and it came without any one of them having a monster day.

Benjamin Hall was the best of them on the fewest snaps. He ran nine times for 81 yards, a 9.0 average, and added four catches for 26 yards, 107 total on 24 snaps. The 39-yard touchdown came on a third-and-9 from the shotgun, with Hall breaking tackles and the Tar Heels never trailing again. He did not start, and he left the game with an injury at some point. I don't know how serious it is, and it matters because Louisville is next.

Demon June was the most-used back at 30 snaps. He carried 15 times for 60 yards (4.0) and caught two passes for 31, including the 23-yarder that was Carolina's longest completion of the day. He did not score after coming in with a team-high eight rushing touchdowns.

Jaylen McGill, the listed starter, played 22 snaps and ran seven times for 15 yards (2.1) with three catches for 18. He is the one who needs to find more. The three backs also caught nine passes for 75 yards between them.

The run game was not asked to carry the offense, and it didn't, but it was productive enough to keep UConn honest. Hall's explosiveness is the difference between a B and a B+, and his health is the biggest question for the group.

Wide receiversB

This is the group with the thinnest box score. The receivers combined for 11 catches and 104 yards on Edwards's 29 completions, and the tight ends and running backs caught 18 of them between them.

Jordan Shipp played 72 snaps and caught five passes for 55 yards and a touchdown, his fourth straight game with a touchdown catch. The 6-yarder after the fumble recovery was his, and so was the fourth-and-5 conversion that extended the late drive. He is the one Edwards trusts in tight spots. He didn't have a catch longer than 18 yards.

Trech Kekahuna played 49 snaps and caught four for 29 yards with a long of 15. Jaden Jefferson had one snap and one catch for 15 yards. Those three accounted for 10 of the group's 11 catches.

In the pre-game breakdown I said Carolina's size would win on the outside against UConn's smaller cornerbacks. It didn't show up in the box score. The longest pass play of the day was 23 yards, which means Edwards never tested UConn's corners over the top, and when the offense is working off the tight ends and backs it doesn't have to.

I'm giving them a B because they did their jobs on the plays that scored and moved the chains when asked, Shipp was reliable in the big spots, and the offense didn't need more. It isn't higher because 11 catches and 104 yards, with nothing longer than 23 yards on the day, is thin, and the group will be asked to do more against teams that don't turn it over three times.

Tight endsA-

Jelani Thurman played 65 of 76 snaps and caught seven passes for 64 yards and two touchdowns, with a long of 20. He now has 45 catches for 525 yards and eight touchdowns through nine games, and the touchdown count ties Mike Chatham's 1979 school record for a tight end in a season. Carolina has three regular-season games left, so the record is his to take alone. Thurman said he was grateful to share it in his first season in Chapel Hill.

Both touchdowns came from 3 yards out, the first with 24 seconds left in the half to end the 16-play drive and the second with 3:38 to play to seal the game. He has been Edwards's most reliable target near the goal line all season.

The rest of the room had a quieter day. Carson Sneed played 18 snaps and caught one pass for 8 yards. Jordan Washington, a listed starter, played 14 snaps with no catches. Shamar Easter, who came in averaging more than 17 yards a catch, played nine snaps and had one catch for 4. Peter Pesansky played four snaps.

Add up the snaps and the five tight ends and fullbacks combined for 110 on 76 offensive plays, which tells you how heavy Carolina's personnel was. The group blocked for a run game that averaged 5.0 a carry from its backs and gave Edwards a place to go in the middle of the field, which was target No. 2 in the pre-game breakdown.

The A- is for the production and the usage. The only knock is that Washington and Easter were not more involved, and Thurman can't be the only one.

Offensive lineB

The five starters barely left the field. Jordan Hall, Christo Kelly and Andrew Threatt played all 76 snaps, Jac'Qawn McRoy played 75 and true freshman Cristian Alvarez played 69. Aidan Banfield got seven snaps and Eidan Buchanan got one. That kind of continuity is rare for this line, and it paid off.

The pre-game worry was left tackle. Hall had a 54.8 grade against Miami and had been in and out of the lineup with injuries, and UConn's best pass rushers play on the edge. He played every snap and Edwards was sacked once, by Esean Carter. Edwards completed 76 percent of his passes, which doesn't happen behind a bad pocket.

The run blocking was better than the numbers it has been putting up. The Tar Heels rushed for 164 yards and 4.6 a carry, and the backs averaged 5.0 against 3.65 for the team on the season. Hall's 39-yarder is the one everyone saw, but the line also held up through the 16-play, 75-yard drive before the half.

The blemish was the penalties. Two of Carolina's six flags were holding calls on the line, and both killed drives. They didn't cost the Tar Heels the game, but they came against a front that produced one sack.

I'm giving the line a B. The sack total and the run average were good, and the work against UConn's pass rush was better than I expected. The holds, and the 13-play drive in the fourth quarter that gained 30 yards, keep it from a B+.

Defensive lineB+

Ten linemen got snaps, and nobody played more than 57. Melkart Abou Jaoude led the way at 57, followed by Jaylen Harvey (52), Leroy Jackson and Isaiah Johnson (38 each) and Xavier Lewis (36). Donovan Hoilette, Calvin Thomas, Nicco Maggio, David Jackson and Emmanuel Nwaiwu rotated for between seven and 13 apiece.

The group combined for 20 tackles and one sack, and the numbers undersell its effect. The Tar Heels held UConn to 123 rushing yards, 4.0 a carry, against an offense that came in averaging 190.8 and 5.32. The longest run by a UConn back was 14 yards. Jackson led the line with five tackles and Johnson had four, and Bowers got his 68 yards and a touchdown without ever breaking a long run.

Abou Jaoude was the best of them. He played 57 snaps and had three tackles, a sack and a tackle for loss, and the line's only sack was his. Hoilette added a tackle for loss in 13 snaps. Harvey played 52 snaps for two tackles, which is a quiet day for a starting end.

Lewis is the story I would watch. He played 36 snaps and had no tackles. He came into the season as the best pass-rush story in the ACC and had seven sacks coming in, but by my count of the game logs he has one in his last six games. Offenses are planning for him, and on Saturday he never got home.

That is where the pass rush came from: Abou Jaoude, and Seelmann from the second level. It was enough on Saturday because UConn's line has given up pressure all year. I'll give the line a B+, with an A- against the run and a B- as a pass rush.

LinebackersA

Only three linebackers played, and all three mattered. Peyton Seelmann played 63 snaps and had nine tackles (six solo), two sacks, two tackles for loss and three pass breakups. It was one of the best games by a Carolina linebacker this season, and it included the fourth-and-19 sack that ended UConn's last drive.

Derek McDonald played 56 snaps and finished with seven tackles, two of them solo. He appeared to hurt his right shoulder in the second quarter and missed very little time, which matters because Carolina has so little depth there. Jonathan Agumadu was out, and Timir Hickman-Collins was the only reserve who played.

Hickman-Collins is my pick for the game's most valuable player. He played 15 snaps and had a pick-six, a fumble recovery and two tackles, and the first two were worth 14 points. The pick-six was a 62-yard return of a tipped pass, and the fumble recovery was the kind of play that comes from being in the right place, which he called instinct.

The group's work in coverage held up, too. UConn's backs and tight ends combined for three catches and 6 yards, and the Huskies' passing game worked on the outside and downfield. Seelmann's three pass breakups and Hickman-Collins's interception came from the second level, and the linebackers were not a problem in the middle of the field the way they were against Miami.

This is the group I said I held the least firmly in the pre-game breakdown, and it turned into the best one on the defense. The thin depth is the one concern, and I'd want McDonald's shoulder checked.

Defensive backsB

Eight defensive backs played. Jaiden Patterson (67 snaps), Greg Smith III (62), Kaleb Cost (46), Javion Butts (44), Coleman Bryson (41), Jalon Thompson (32), Kenton Dopson (20) and Ade Willie (5) shared the work. Peyton Waters was out, and Tre Miller did not appear in the snap counts.

The result was the best coverage day of the season on the surface. UConn completed 17 of 35 passes (48.6 percent), well below the 65 percent opponents had been completing against Carolina, for 245 yards and two touchdowns, and Osborne threw two interceptions. Thompson's was the second of them, his first career interception and the one that set up the clinching touchdown.

The cracks were in the big plays. UConn averaged 14.4 yards a completion and hit a 33-yarder to Julian Allen, a 28-yarder to Xavier Watson and touchdowns of 18 yards to Ryder Treadway and 24 to Cam Abshire. Treadway had four catches for 82 yards. The defense gave up 13 plays of more than 15 yards in all, and missed tackles and blown coverages did most of the damage.

Individually, Cost had five tackles, all of them solo, in 46 snaps. Butts broke up a fourth-and-12 pass that ended UConn's 14-play drive and had three tackles. Patterson had two tackles and a pass breakup, and I'll come back to him below. Smith, Bryson and Thompson each had two or three tackles, and Dopson and Willie did not record a stat.

Patterson deserves more space than his stat line, because he has been the weak spot in this secondary for most of the year. He is a junior and the Tar Heels' top outside corner, he had 34 tackles, five pass breakups and an interception last season, and he came back for 2026 saying he wanted to help build the team. The season has not gone that way. At Clemson he allowed three catches for 55 yards, including a 41-yarder to Bryant Wesco, and missed three tackles in space. Against Notre Dame he led the defense with 71 snaps and was beaten in coverage at times. Against Miami, a pass interference flag on a third-and-1 inside the 5, which came right after he had broken up the pass, gave the Hurricanes first-and-goal, and Mark Fletcher scored from 2 yards on the next play to make it 24-21. Belichick was furious about the call, and some thought it was questionable, but it is still the kind of play that keeps showing up next to his name.

Saturday was better than those games. He played 67 snaps, more than any other Tar Heel defensive back, and finished with two tackles and a breakup. I can't tell you from the box score who was in coverage on each of UConn's long completions, so I am not going to pin any of them on him. What I can say is that the Huskies hit 13 plays of more than 15 yards, and a corner who is already shaky does not get the benefit of the doubt on those. Freshman Kenton Dopson played 20 snaps with no stat, and how that number moves over the last three games is worth watching.

A B is right. The takeaways and the completion percentage are the best they have been, and the group did what it was asked to do against a quarterback who can make plays. The explosive plays are what keep this from a B+, and Waters and Miller are the question for Louisville.

Special teamsB-

Rece Verhoff made a 27-yard field goal in the third quarter and all five extra points, and he missed a 49-yard attempt wide left with 8:07 to play in the fourth. That one cost nothing, since UConn did not score again, but it was a chance to make it a two-score game. It was a long try in the rain.

Aeron Burrell punted three times for 126 yards, a 42.0 average, with a long of 47 and one inside the 20. Two of his three punts came in the first quarter. UConn's Tommy Warner punted twice for a 48.0 average with a long of 55, so the Huskies won that exchange, but there were only five punts in the game.

Returns were quiet. Jaden Jefferson returned one kickoff for 14 yards, and UNC had no punt returns. UConn's Ky Wilson returned five kickoffs for 97 yards, a 19.4 average with a long of 24. Coverage was fine. There was one flag, an illegal formation penalty on a punt.

The pre-game edge I gave Carolina on special teams was small, and it was about right. UNC's kicker was 1 for 2 and UConn's was 0 for 1 with a miss from 45, and neither return game did anything. The B- is for a missed field goal and a flag, and for the fact that nothing went badly wrong.

CoachingA-

Belichick said the team bounced back after a couple of tough losses with a solid week of practice, and that the program is definitely ahead of where it was a year ago. The scoreboard backs up the first. The standings back up the second, since the win surpasses last season's total of four.

The staff deserves credit for the turnover emphasis. UNC practices it every day, and it produced three takeaways in a game after two without one. The third-and-9 call that Hall turned into a touchdown and the fourth-and-5 call to Shipp were both good, and UNC went 2 for 2 on fourth down.

The defensive staff held UConn to 21 points and 123 rushing yards and rotated 10 linemen. It had only three linebackers available because Agumadu was out, and it managed that without a problem.

Carolina has now scored 30 or more in three straight games after failing to reach 30 against an FBS team in its first 19 under Belichick. The offense is more reliable than it was. I'd question the second-half drought and the 13-play drive that gained 30 yards and ended in a missed 49-yard try.

Discipline is the one real mark. Six penalties for 50 yards from a team that is normally among the cleanest in the conference is a coaching issue, and so are the two holds. I'd give the staff an A- for the result and the response, and the penalties and second-half offense are the reasons it isn't an A.

Who stood out and who needs more

Five who stood out

1

Timir Hickman-Collins, linebacker

Fifteen snaps, a pick-six, a fumble recovery and two tackles. The sophomore tipped Osborne's pass and returned it 62 yards for his first career touchdown, then fell on the Osborne fumble that led to Shipp's touchdown. Peyton Seelmann, linebacker

Nine tackles, two sacks, two tackles for loss and three pass breakups on 63 snaps. The final sack, on fourth-and-19, ended UConn's last drive. Seelmann has been a good player for the Tar Heels all year, and this was the game where the production matched the snaps.

Seven catches for 64 yards and two touchdowns, and a share of the school record for touchdown catches by a tight end. He is up to 45 catches and 525 yards for the season, and the tight end position is where Carolina's offense lives.

Jalon Thompson, cornerback

The junior's first career interception came with 7:15 left and the Tar Heels up 31-21, and it led to the touchdown that put the game away. He played 32 snaps and had two tackles, and he got his hands on a ball when Carolina needed one.

Four who need more

1

Jaiden Patterson, cornerback

Sixty-seven snaps, two tackles and a pass breakup is a fine line for a corner. This has been a hard year for the junior. Clemson went at him for a 41-yard completion and three missed tackles, Notre Dame beat him at times, and the pass interference call at Miami set up the touchdown that put the Hurricanes ahead 24-21. He is still the Tar Heels' top outside corner, and the defense needs him to be that against Louisville's receivers. If Dopson's snaps keep going up, it will be because the coaches are seeing the same thing.

2

Xavier Lewis, defensive end

Thirty-six snaps and no tackles. Lewis has been the face of Carolina's pass rush, and he has one sack in his last six games by my count. The Tar Heels got to Osborne anyway, but they will need Lewis to start winning again before they face better offensive lines.

3

Jaylen McGill, running back

The listed starter had seven carries for 15 yards in 22 snaps, a 2.1 average, and three catches for 18. McGill has made big plays this year, including the winning touchdown at Duke, and the Tar Heels need that version of him back.

4

Rece Verhoff, kicker

He made a 27-yard field goal and all five extra points, but the 49-yard miss wide left with 8:07 to play was a chance to make it a two-score game. It didn't matter on Saturday. Carolina will not always be this lucky.

How my pre-game read held up

Since I put my read on the record before the game, here is how it held up. I had North Carolina 31, UConn 17. The final was 38-21, so I had the winner, the margin range and the general shape right, and I was low on both teams. The pre-game breakdown also said 31-17 was the optimistic end of where this could land. It wasn't.

Of the six targets I listed, four hit. Osborne's protection was the big one, with three sacks, two interceptions and a lost fumble. The tight ends worked the middle of the field and Thurman scored twice. Carolina went 2 for 2 on fourth down, and it took the ball away three times and cashed in all three for touchdowns. The two that missed were the outside receivers, who had 11 catches for 104 yards, and UConn's penalties. I called the Huskies' 8.5 flags a game a target, and they took three.

The concerns were mixed. I worried about the raw numbers, and the yardage was nearly even, 419 to 368, with 5.7 yards a play to 5.6. I worried about big plays and tackling in space, and the Huskies had a 93-yard touchdown drive and 13 plays of 15 or more yards. I worried about finishing, and the Tar Heels did. I worried about turnovers, and they gave it away once. Left tackle was fine, the secondary was better than I feared without Waters, and the hangover never showed.

On the position groups, I called the running backs even and UNC's backs averaged 5.0 a carry to UConn's 4.75 for Bowers and Christian. I gave the linebackers to UNC and said it was the edge I held least firmly, and it was the best group on the field. I had the offensive line slightly to Carolina, the defensive line to Carolina and the quarterbacks slightly to Carolina, and all three held up. The one I missed was receivers: I gave UNC the edge there, and the numbers say UConn's group was more productive, with Treadway at 82 yards and Abshire's touchdown.

The game script was off in the details. I had it 7-7 after the first quarter, 17-10 at the half, 24-10 after three and 31-17 at the end. It was 7-0, 28-14, 31-21 and 38-21. I had Carolina winning the second quarter and holding on in the fourth, and that was right, but I underestimated the second quarter, when UNC scored 21. I predicted an explosive run from Cornist or Christian and neither had one. I predicted a UConn turnover in the third quarter, and they came in the first, second and fourth.

The thing I would change is how much I weighted UConn's offense. I leaned on its season averages, but its losses had come with big defensive breakdowns, and the turnover margin was always going to matter. The Tar Heels won it 3-1, and everything else followed. I also wrote that the weather was not a factor, and it rained for most of the game.

What's next

North Carolina is 5-4 (2-3 ACC) and has three games left. Louisville (5-3) comes to Chapel Hill on Nov. 14, UNC goes to Virginia on Nov. 21, and NC State visits on Nov. 28 for Senior Day. The Wolfpack are 5-4 after a 48-36 upset of Georgia Tech and have a bye and a trip to Miami before they get to Kenan Stadium. UConn (4-5) hosts James Madison on Nov. 14.

Six wins makes a team bowl eligible, and Carolina is one win away. Seven takes two wins in the final three games, and eight takes a sweep. That is a very different position from the one a loss would have left. At 4-5, the Tar Heels would have needed two of the last three just to get to six, and seven would have required a sweep.

The health picture matters now that the games are ACC games and the conference's availability report applies. Hall left the UConn game with an injury, Waters and Agumadu missed it, and McDonald appeared to hurt his right shoulder and came back. The offensive line came through healthy, and Hickman-Collins was the only reserve linebacker who played.

There are things to fix before Louisville. The penalties need to go back to where they were, the defense has to tackle better in space, and the offense needs to figure out why it went quiet after halftime. The receivers outside the tight ends also have to do more, because Louisville is unlikely to hand over three takeaways and a pick-six.

I wouldn't count on three takeaways again. What should carry over is the rest of it: the tight ends working the middle, a run game that can average 5 a carry, a front that can get after the quarterback, and Edwards playing as efficiently as he did Saturday. If the Tar Heels do those things, one win in three is a reasonable ask, and two is a real possibility.

There are four things I would watch against Louisville. The first is Hall's status, because the run game is better with him than without him. The second is whether the penalties go back to normal, since six flags for 50 yards is not who the Tar Heels have been. The third is whether the offense that scored touchdowns on three straight second-quarter drives can sustain it into the third and fourth quarters, which was the issue in the Clemson and Duke games and showed up again on Saturday. The fourth is Patterson, and whether he can have a clean game on the outside or Dopson's share of the snaps keeps growing.
